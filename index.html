Start here

HVAC systems all do the same big jobs: provide heating, cooling, ventilation (fresh air), and filtration. The difference is where the air is conditioned (at the zone vs. centrally) and how the energy is moved (ducted air vs. refrigerant piping vs. hydronic piping).

Rooftop Unit (RTU)

A packaged unit that heats/cools air and delivers it through ductwork. Common for retail and offices.

Fan Coil Unit (FCU)

A small zone unit that uses a fan + coil (water or DX refrigerant) to condition a room or small area.

VRF

A heat pump system that moves refrigerant to many indoor units. Great zoning and efficiency potential.

DOAS

A dedicated outdoor air system that handles ventilation air (often humidity too). Commonly paired with VRF or fan coils.

Quick comparison

High-level differences (not every design case)
System Main energy moved Typical strength Common trade-off
RTU Air (ducts) Simple, packaged, widely used Zoning can be limited; duct space needed
Fan Coil Water (pipes) + Air (in-zone fan) Good room-by-room control Needs a ventilation strategy (often DOAS)
VRF Refrigerant (pipes) Excellent zoning; heat recovery options Design/controls more complex; refrigerant safety
DOAS Air (ventilation) Ventilation + humidity control done centrally Usually not the only heating/cooling system

Tip: In real projects, systems are often combined (ex: DOAS + VRF or DOAS + fan coils) so each piece does what it’s best at.
